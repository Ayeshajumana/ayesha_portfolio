LASER CUTTING

Comprehensive technical documentation from digital preparation and RDWorks layer assignment to precision CO₂ acrylic fabrication.

Section 01

1. Lab Safety & Safety Rules

Operational safety protocols and machine subsystem checks followed during laser cutting.

RULE 01 • OPTICAL

Laser Safety

Class 4 enclosed laser system operated strictly with the interlocked protective lid closed. Direct viewing of active beam path avoided.

RULE 02 • VENTILATION

Exhaust System

High-cfm centrifugal exhaust blower actively running to extract vaporized acrylic fumes, odors, and particulate through outdoor ducting.

RULE 03 • THERMAL

Chiller Operation

Industrial water chiller (CW-5200) verified prior to firing to ensure continuous deionized coolant circulation at 18–22°C around the CO₂ tube.

RULE 04 • ELECTRICAL

Chassis Earthing

Direct low-resistance earth ground verified on the laser frame to prevent electrostatic charge accumulation and protect high-voltage power supplies.

RULE 05 • PNEUMATICS

Air Assist

Coaxial compressed air jet active at the nozzle to extinguish combustion flare-ups, eject molten kerf debris, and prevent lens contamination.

RULE 06 • PROTOCOL

General Machine Safety

Never leaving the laser unattended during operation. Emergency Stop switch verified and dedicated CO₂ fire extinguisher within arm's reach.

Section 04

4. Selected Design / Image

Selection rationale for multi-operational laser fabrication.

Batman Emblem & Hot Wheels Keychains

Selection Reason: The Batman emblem and Hot Wheels logo were selected as ideal benchmark test geometries combining two distinct digital fabrication operations in a single workpiece:

  • Outer Perimeter: Complex organic curves and sharp acute internal corners testing beam acceleration and through-thickness cut separation.
  • Interior Typography & Infill: Intricate raster typography testing surface scan/engraving definition without compromising thin wall structural rigidity.
  • Functional Feature: Integrated 3.5 mm mounting eyelet hole for keyring assembly.

5. Image-to-DXF Conversion

Vectorization pipeline from raster graphic to mathematical CAD geometry.

STEP 01

Software Tool

Adobe Illustrator & Inkscape (Bitmap Trace Engine) used to segment high-contrast silhouette boundaries into Bezier curves.

STEP 02

Conversion Process

Applied threshold luminance edge detection, converted bitmap pixels to clean vector polylines, and resolved intersecting anchor nodes.

STEP 03

DXF Export

Exported as AutoCAD R12/2000 DXF format in millimeters (1:1 unit scale) to preserve exact geometric tolerances for RDWorks.

Section 06

6. File Preparation

Geometry verification, path closure, and vector cleanup before machining.

PHASE A

Vector Cleaning & Node Reduction

Removed redundant stray nodes and micro-segments along curved sections. Reduced curve noise to ensure smooth, continuous gantry acceleration without stuttering.

PHASE B

Closed Path Verification

Executed "Check Closed Curves" in CAD/CAM to verify every perimeter contour is 100% closed, preventing incomplete cutouts during laser execution.

PHASE C

Scaling & Dimensioning

Scaled Batman emblem to 60 mm width × 35 mm height; scaled Hot Wheels emblem to 75 mm width × 42 mm height with 3.5 mm keychain eyelet holes.

PHASE D

Duplicate Geometry Removal

Ran "Delete Overlap" in RDWorks to eliminate double lines along shared borders, preventing localized double-burning and edge melting.

Section 11

11. Problems Faced & Solutions

Engineering troubleshooting and defect remediation log.

Problem Encountered Identified Cause Solution Implemented Final Outcome
Excessive edge melting & kerf flare Cutting speed too low, causing high heat buildup in 3.0 mm acrylic. Increased feed speed from 60 mm/s to 100 mm/s at 30% power. Clean, sharp flame-polished optical edges without warping.
Part dropped before engraving finished Outer Cut layer was configured with higher priority than Scan layer. Reordered layers in RDWorks: Blue Scan set to Priority 1, Black Cut set to Priority 2. Engraving completed perfectly on stable sheet before final drop.
Faint engraving depth on lettering Scan speed was excessive (400 mm/s) for the low tube wattage. Reduced scan speed to 250 mm/s with 20% power. Crisp, uniform raster contrast across all typography.
Section 12

12. Reflection

Key learning outcomes, skills acquired, and future process improvements.

LEARNING • SKILLS

What I Learned & Skills Gained

Mastered the end-to-end digital-to-physical CAM pipeline for CO₂ flatbed laser cutters. Gained hands-on competency in optical path alignment principles, Ruida DSP controller operation, RDWorks layer hierarchy (Cut vs Scan sequencing), and focal distance calibration using stepped focal tools.

ITERATION • FUTURE

What I Would Do Differently

In future fabrications, I will utilize fine-grit paper masking on both acrylic faces to completely eliminate electrostatic smoke condensation. I will also incorporate kerf-offset compensation ($+0.12\text{ mm}$) into the CAD vector model for press-fit mating interlocking enclosures.
